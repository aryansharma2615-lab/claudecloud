# FarmHand motion hardware + control stack — what to buy and what runs the motion (researched 2026-10-02)

**How to read this.** Grades: A = measured/tested; B = built + photos/video; C = vendor spec; D = hearsay/forum/issue/recall. `[f]` = I fetched and read the source (GitHub raw files, vendor PDF on GitHub, PyPI JSON); `[s]` = number comes from a web-search result summary of the linked page (page itself blocked by the sandbox proxy: **verify before it enters a BOM**). `[d]` = derived by me (arithmetic from cited numbers). `[r]` = my recall, no source, grade D.
**Limits of this pass.** Shop and vendor pages (stepperonline, Amazon.ca, Makerstore.cc, Digi-Key, AliExpress detail, HIWIN, Mean Well, ODrive/mjbots docs) were not fetchable, and the session's 200-search budget ran out before rails, brakes, PSU and Pi/N100 were searched. Those rows say "not documented" rather than guess. Prices: currency as listed on the search snapshot 2026-10-02 (listing dates not shown); `~C$` uses an **assumed** 1 USD = 1.40 CAD, 1 EUR = 1.60 CAD (no FX source fetched).
**Acronyms.** FOC = field-oriented control; BLDC = brushless DC; QDD = quasi-direct drive (BLDC + low-ratio planetary); CAN / CAN-FD = controller area network (flexible data rate); PPR/CPR = pulses / counts per revolution; INL = integral non-linearity (encoder angle error); SSI = synchronous serial interface; MCU = microcontroller; PSU = power supply; arcmin = 1/60 degree; arcsec = 1/3600 degree; HTD/GT2 = belt tooth profiles; TMC = Trinamic driver chip family; IK = inverse kinematics; TOPP-RA = time-optimal path parametrisation; NC = normally closed; ESU = Extended Security Updates; MCPWM/PCNT/RMT = ESP32 pulse-generation peripherals; CF-PA = carbon-fibre polyamide; PLA / PETG / PC = polylactic acid / glycol-modified PET / polycarbonate filaments; CA = cyanoacrylate glue; ALM = driver alarm output.

**Numbers that decide the architecture** `[d]`:
1. ±1 mm at a 0.5 m reach = ±0.115° = **±6.9 arcmin for the whole joint chain**. A 20 arcmin planetary alone is 2.9 mm. So any joint with a planetary gearbox needs an **output-side** absolute encoder, and gravity joints want a reducer ≤ 1 arcmin class or a gravity-preloaded backlash.
2. Payload alone: 1.5 kg × 9.81 × 0.5 m = 7.4 N·m. The brief's 25–40 N·m shoulder includes arm mass (assumption carried from the brief).
3. A stepper behind a 50:1 reducer can push **motor torque × 50** unless current is capped: 3 N·m × 50 = 150 N·m = 300 N at 0.5 m. Closed-loop does not limit that; the current setting does.
4. Encoder resolution is not accuracy: every 0.1° of INL is 0.87 mm at 0.5 m. INL figures were **not** fetched; get them from the datasheets before choosing.

**What "closed loop" really detects** (C unless noted):
- Motor-shaft encoder only. The CL57T raises a **position-following-error alarm** (causes listed: encoder wiring, insufficient torque, speed too high, stall) `[s]` [manual](https://www.omc-stepperonline.com/download/CL57T_V4.0.pdf). It sees nothing after the reducer: backlash, belt stretch, slip, structure flex and collision force are invisible. That is why joints need an output-side absolute encoder (moteus documents the same dual-encoder pattern: rotor encoder for commutation, output encoder for absolute position `[f]` [dual-encoders.md](https://github.com/mjbots/moteus/blob/main/docs/guides/dual-encoders.md)).
- MKS stall protection has two independent modes, **both default off**: over-current, and "position error > y counts within x × 15 ms". Example in the manual: 14000 counts (180°) in 300 ms. **On trigger the motor unlocks and releases the shaft**; it is cleared by command 3DH, Enter key, or loosening the shaft `[f]` (manual §5.4). A gravity joint therefore drops its arm on a stall trip unless a brake holds it. The heartbeat watchdog (§5.2.15, default off) stops the motor if no command arrives within the set time `[f]`.
- Pull-out torque falls with speed and the closed-loop drive does not move that curve; it only lets you run on it without a margin `[s]` [linearmotiontips](https://www.linearmotiontips.com/how-does-closed-loop-stepper-control-work/). One summary reports a 24 V setup losing >60% of low-speed torque by 600 rpm while 48 V/70 V held (source page unidentified, D) `[s]` [Teknic](https://teknic.com/selecting-power-supply/). StepperOnline's torque-curve PDF for 23HS45 was not fetched: **24 V pull-out at 300/600 rpm is not documented here**.
- Failure reports: see "Documented failures" below (MKS issue tracker, BTT S42C threads, Forte arm skipping steps).

## 5. Brakes (NEMA 17/23, 24 V)
- Price, torque and model data for stepper power-off brakes: **not documented** (no page fetched). Known indirect facts: JMC iHSV57 has a brake version (36 V, 0.65 N·m); MyActuator sells brake variants; MKS drives release the shaft on stall.
- Sizing `[d]`: a brake on the **motor** shaft sees load/ratio: 25–40 N·m ÷ 50 = 0.5–0.8 N·m (ideal), so a 1.2–2 N·m class NEMA 23 brake covers it; verify the actual rating. Engage only at zero speed except on E-stop (shock load on the reducer).
- Do gravity joints need them? Yes, for R7 "no stalled-motor holds": without a brake the only way to hold is to energise the motor (the stall hold R7 forbids) or accept a fall on any fault/E-stop/stall trip. Brake coil supply must run through the E-stop contactor so power loss closes the brake. Horizontal joints (wrist roll, rail axis, gripper) do not need one.

## 6. Linear axis (7th axis or gantry, ~1.5 m)
Rail load ratings (MGN12/15, HGR15/20), price per metre (Chinese vs HIWIN), extrusion deflection (2040/2060/4040), belt vs rack vs lead/ball screw speed/accuracy/cost: **not documented, blocked by search budget and proxy**. Only sourced point: PAROL6 (HTD 3M, 6 mm) and Forte (timing belts) use belts at small scale `[f]`/`[s]`. Judgement (no source): belt for speed, rack-and-pinion or screw where ±1 mm over 1.5 m must hold; either way close the loop with an absolute linear reference (limit switch + AprilTag recalibration, R10), and size the carriage on a rail, not on extrusion slots.

- **Firmware options.** Own C++ (FastAccelStepper / hardware timers) is the only path that gives per-joint output-encoder loops, brake outputs, ALM inputs and a JSON-lines protocol. grblHAL: 300 kHz pulses, G-code, encoder module, a kinematics folder `[f]` [core](https://github.com/grblHAL/core), but Teensy 4.1 board files cap at 5 axes and closed-loop is not core. FluidNC: no arm kinematics or feedback. **Klipper** has no articulated-arm kinematics (list: cartesian, delta, deltesian, corexy, corexz, hybrid, polar, rotary_delta, winch, generic_cartesian, none) and needs a Linux host `[f]`. **PAROL6 firmware + server** is the closest working template: 100 Hz Python control loop, 3 Mbaud serial with 58-byte frames, hardware E-stop pin checked every tick → DISABLE `[f]` [PAROL6 Python API](https://github.com/PCrnjak/PAROL6-python-API). AR2 / Annin Robot Project: Arduino sketch for stepper drivers + Python (Tk) UI, geared steppers from StepperOnline `[f]` [AR2](https://github.com/Chris-Annin/AR2).
- **Loop rates (documented).** MKS internal loops 10–20 kHz; moteus 15–30 kHz; PAROL6 host 100 Hz with 50 Hz jog stream; this repo's ARCHITECTURE.md specifies 10 Hz heartbeat and SAFETY.md H8 a 500 ms controller watchdog. Proposed split `[d]`: PC plans and sends setpoints at 50–100 Hz; MCU runs a 1 kHz joint loop (SPI encoder read at ≥1 MHz, step trains to CL57T, alarm and brake pins); drives run their own fast loops.

## 9. 24 V power and E-stop
- Reference build: PAROL6 uses "any 24 V 5 A" supply (Mean Well EDR-120-24, 120 W DIN) for six NEMA 17 axes with TMC5160 drivers, an NC E-stop whose pin the controller polls every tick and answers with a firmware DISABLE (whether it also cuts motor power is not documented), GX16 power connector `[f]` [BOM](https://github.com/PCrnjak/PAROL6-Desktop-robot-arm/blob/main/BOM/BOM.md), [API](https://github.com/PCrnjak/PAROL6-python-API). That is weaker than SAFETY.md H11 (NC E-stop in series with driver power, category 0), which FarmHand should keep.
- Mean Well LRS-350-24 / RSP-500-24 specs, prices, safety-relay or contactor part numbers, fuse ratings: **not documented** (blocked). Recall only, D, unlinked: LRS-350-24 ≈ 350 W / 14.6 A; RSP-500-24 ≈ 500 W / 21 A with PFC `[r]`.
- Supply rules from moteus docs `[f]` [electrical-setup.md](https://github.com/mjbots/moteus/blob/main/docs/guides/electrical-setup.md): connect everything before powering (no hot-plug of XT30), use pre-charge or a soft-start supply, solder joints must be sound (sparking → transients). **A normal PSU cannot absorb regenerative power: bus voltage can rise without bound, failing CAN transceivers on every device and, severe, the FETs.** Limit accel, set regen limits, or add brake resistor/clamp. Applies equally to MKS (30 V FETs on 57D) and CL57T.
- Pattern `[d]`: PSU → fuse/breaker per branch → **contactor (or safety relay) coil in the NC E-stop loop** → motor-driver V+ and brake coils; logic 24 V → 5 V/3.3 V stays live so MCU and output encoders keep position (absolute output encoders mean no re-home after E-stop). ALM outputs from CL57T and MKS stall flag go to MCU inputs and to the supervisor.
